## Business Context

Intellimation.ai already had an established brand identity and website when Jessica joined in March 2025, but the marketing *function* was still evolving. As the business expanded its portfolio of Vertical AI solutions for financial services, the commercial team was engaging more enterprise prospects — running multiple product demonstrations, proofs of concept (POCs), and client meetings every week. The technology itself was strong, but there was a communication gap: product positioning wasn't consistent, and there was no comprehensive set of enterprise-ready marketing and sales assets to support those conversations.

Because Intellimation operated in financial services, the audience was highly specialised — investment banks, capital markets firms, and other financial institutions. The challenge was never "make it look good"; it was building credibility with a technical, skeptical, senior buyer, and translating sophisticated AI capabilities into business-focused communication they'd trust.

## Objectives

- Bridge the gap between a fast-moving enterprise sales motion and inconsistent product messaging.
- Develop clearer, more consistent product positioning across all four Vertical AI product lines.
- Create the materials the commercial team needed throughout the enterprise sales journey (not just "collateral" — a system).
- Equip sales and leadership with consistent, credible resources that build confidence with prospective enterprise clients.
- Translate complex, technical AI capabilities into commercial narrative business decision-makers (not engineers) would act on.

## Product Marketing Process

### Discovery & Research
Every brochure / positioning exercise began with a **product discovery session** — Jessica, the CEO, the Director of AI Products, and relevant subject matter experts. Goal: understand not just how the product worked, but *why it mattered to customers*. Used a structured product marketing framework to interrogate: target audience, customer pain points, value proposition, competitive differentiation, key features, use cases, commercial objectives.

### Customer Research / Voice of the Customer (VoC)
Jessica personally attended product demos, POCs, and client meetings, and documented meeting notes, recurring questions, objections, and feedback in real time. This became a **structured question bank and knowledge repository** — the company's first formal Voice of the Customer programme.

- **700+ customer questions** collected from enterprise sales meetings, demonstrations, and POCs.
- Categorised into themes: **technical capabilities, compliance and risk, commercial value, AI accuracy, deployment, data infrastructure.**
- Insights shared across marketing, product, engineering, and sales — used to refine positioning, strengthen messaging, improve demo storylines, and ensure future content addressed the questions customers were actually asking.
- Treated as a living system, not a one-off research project — brochures and presentations were continuously revised based on real buyer feedback rather than treated as static documents.
- **Evidence status:** the original VoC database itself was retained by the company and is not accessible (NDA) — but the full methodology (structure, categorisation themes, how it fed other teams) is fully known and can be described/recreated in diagram form.

### Messaging Framework
Discovery + VoC input were translated into a structured messaging framework per product line — the single foundation reused across brochures, solution briefs, website copy, LinkedIn thought leadership, sales presentations, and email campaigns. This is the mechanism that gave Intellimation consistent positioning across every touchpoint instead of bespoke messaging invented per opportunity.

### Product Positioning / Value Proposition
Positioning work translated complex, technical AI capabilities (e.g. margin booking automation, prime brokerage AI agents, legal agreement intelligence, KYC lifecycle automation) into value propositions a financial-services buyer — heads of quant, CTOs, senior bank executives — would find credible and relevant, prioritising business outcomes over technical architecture.

### Go-to-Market Support / Sales Enablement
Once approved, messaging and assets became part of the formal sales enablement toolkit — used across product demonstrations, POCs, client meetings, conferences, and broader business development. Jessica also **coordinated and directly supported a Proof of Concept (POC)**, giving her first-hand insight into how product marketing, product, and sales collaborate during actual enterprise implementations (not just the messaging that leads up to one).

### Production & Review
Jessica owned both content **and** visual design — building brochures, solution briefs, presentations, and sales collateral herself in **Figma**, aligning layout to brand while making complex technical information legible. Assets went through several review rounds with the CEO and Director of AI Products, focused on technical accuracy and commercial positioning; Jessica refined messaging, structure, and visual presentation in response.

## Lessons Learned

- The strongest marketing begins before anything is designed — it starts with listening to customers, identifying patterns in feedback, and translating those into a value proposition every team can communicate consistently.
- Product marketing's job is not to produce content; it's to understand customer needs and use those insights to continuously improve positioning, messaging, and the customer experience.
- Working directly with CEO, CFO, Director of AI Products, engineers, and commercial teams taught her how product marketing connects the entire business — bridging highly technical products and commercial storytelling, balancing technical accuracy with customer value.
- Great product marketing is measured by how effectively assets help customers understand the product, support sales conversations, and create alignment across the business — not by the number of assets produced. This systems-thinking approach now shapes how she approaches every marketing project.
- Proudest achievement: establishing a product marketing function — and specifically a Voice of the Customer programme — where none existed before.

## STAR Stories

### STAR 1 — Building the company's first Voice of the Customer programme
- **Situation:** Intellimation had no product marketing function. The commercial team was running roughly five enterprise demos a week across POCs and client meetings, but customer feedback wasn't being captured or used systematically — it lived in individual people's heads and notes.
- **Task:** As the sole product marketer, turn scattered, informal customer feedback into something structured enough to actually improve messaging and align product, engineering, and sales.
- **Action:** Personally attended demos, POCs, and client meetings; documented meeting notes, recurring questions, and objections after every session; built a structured knowledge repository categorising 700+ customer questions into six themes (technical capabilities, compliance and risk, commercial value, AI accuracy, deployment, data infrastructure); shared the synthesised insights across marketing, product, engineering, and sales.
- **Result:** Established the company's first formal Voice of the Customer programme. Insights directly refined product positioning, strengthened messaging, and improved demo storylines — creating a continuous feedback loop so marketing evolved with real buyer conversations instead of assumptions.

### STAR 2 — Building a reusable enterprise messaging system from zero
- **Situation:** Intellimation was expanding across four Vertical AI product lines with no dedicated product marketing function and no consistent positioning. The commercial team lacked enterprise-ready assets to support a fast-moving sales motion.
- **Task:** Create clear, credible product positioning and a scalable library of sales assets the commercial team could actually use throughout the enterprise sales journey.
- **Action:** Ran structured discovery sessions with the CEO, the Director of AI Products, and subject matter experts for each product line; built a repeatable messaging framework (audience, pain points, value proposition, differentiation, features, use cases, commercial objectives); designed brochures and sales collateral herself in Figma; ran review cycles with the CEO and Director of AI Products for technical accuracy and commercial positioning; reused the same messaging framework across brochures, the website, LinkedIn, decks, and email.
- **Result:** Delivered a full enterprise asset library supporting 40–60 demos across four named, enterprise-grade AI products (Margin Booking AI, Prime Brokerage AI Agents, Legal Agreement Intelligence, KYC Lifecycle Automation) — giving the commercial team consistent, credible material for the first time in the company's history.
