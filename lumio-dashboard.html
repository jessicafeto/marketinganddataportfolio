One dashboard, three platforms, and an answer to "is this working?"

After rebuilding paid media (case study 05) and email lifecycle (case study 06), Lumio still couldn't see the two channels side by side. I consolidated Meta Ads, Google Ads, Klaviyo and Shopify data into a single blended reporting view turning a day of manual spreadsheet work each month into a 15-minute check-in.

Sector DTC / e-commerce wellness subscription
Client Lumio (fictional brand)
Role Marketing analyst & dashboard owner
Tools Looker Studio / Power BI demoed here with Chart.js
01 The brief

Three platforms, three definitions of "performance."

With paid media restructured and the email lifecycle programme live, Lumio's leadership had more data than ever spread across Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads, Klaviyo and Shopify, each with its own definitions, date ranges and export formats. Every month, someone manually pulled numbers from each platform into a spreadsheet to answer one question: is marketing working, and where should the next pound go? The process took most of a day, was error-prone, and produced a report that was already a month out of date by the time it was discussed.

02 The objective

One source of truth, refreshed without manual work.

Consolidate the data

Bring spend, revenue and email performance from all three platforms into one consistent dataset, with shared definitions for what counts as "spend" and "revenue" per channel.

Define the blended KPIs

Agree on the handful of numbers that actually mattered blended CAC, ROAS by channel, and channel revenue mix and calculate them consistently every time.

Make it self-serve

Build a dashboard the founder and team could check themselves, without waiting for a monthly export turning a day of work into a 15-minute review.

03 My role

From scattered exports to one reporting view.

I owned marketing analytics and reporting for Lumio: pulling and reconciling data from Meta Ads, Google Ads, Klaviyo and Shopify, defining how blended CAC and channel-level ROAS should be calculated, and building the dashboard that brought it all together. I also ran the monthly walkthrough with the founder, using the dashboard to ground budget conversations in the same numbers every time.

04 Strategy

Get the definitions right, then make them visible.

A dashboard is only as useful as the numbers feeding it. Most of the effort went into the first pillar below the second followed naturally once the data was trustworthy.

A

Data consolidation & blended metrics

Built a weekly data pull from Meta Ads, Google Ads, Klaviyo and Shopify into a consolidated sheet, with a written definition for every metric what counted as "spend" (ad spend only, vs. ad spend plus tooling costs), what counted as "revenue" per channel (last-click vs. assisted), and how blended CAC and channel ROAS were calculated from those figures. This single source of truth meant every chart on the dashboard, and every conversation about it, used the same numbers.

B

Self-serve reporting

Designed a dashboard around the four questions that came up most often: how is blended CAC trending? Where is revenue actually coming from? Which channels are most efficient? And is the email programme's contribution growing? Each question got one chart, kept deliberately simple, so the founder could check in without needing a walkthrough every time.

05 Execution

From data pipeline to monthly habit.

Data pipeline & definitions

Set up a weekly export routine from each platform into a consolidated Google Sheet, with a short data dictionary defining every field so anyone picking up the sheet later could understand exactly what each number meant and where it came from.

Blended CAC & ROAS calculations

Built the formulas for blended CAC (total spend across paid channels ÷ new customers) and channel-level ROAS (channel revenue ÷ channel spend, with email's "spend" treated as the Klaviyo subscription cost), agreed with the founder before they went anywhere near a chart.

Dashboard build

Built the reporting view itself in practice this would live in Looker Studio or Power BI connected directly to the data sources; for this portfolio it's recreated below as an interactive Chart.js dashboard using the same figures and structure.

Stakeholder rollout

Ran a short walkthrough with the founder covering how to read each chart and what "good" looked like for each metric, then moved to a monthly 15-minute check-in using the dashboard as the agenda.

06 The dashboard

Blended performance, H1.

All charts below are interactive hover over any point or bar for exact figures.

Blended CAC fell from £46 to £29 over the six months shown the same trend reported in the paid media case study, now visible alongside revenue and channel mix rather than on its own.

Email's share of revenue nearly tripled, from around 9% to almost 24% the clearest single signal that the lifecycle flows were working, and one the founder checks first each month.

08 Learnings

What worked, what I'd change next time.

  • 01

    Definitions are the hard part, not the charts.

    Most of the time on this project went into agreeing what "spend" and "revenue" meant per channel not into building visuals. Once those definitions were written down and agreed, the dashboard itself came together quickly.

  • 02

    Fewer charts, used more often, beats more charts, used rarely.

    Early drafts had nearly a dozen charts. Cutting down to four one per recurring question made the dashboard something the founder actually opened each month, rather than something built once and forgotten.

  • 03

    Blended views surface trade-offs single-channel views hide.

    Looking at paid and email side by side made it obvious that email's growing contribution was partly why blended CAC could fall even as paid spend increased a connection that wasn't visible when each channel had its own report.

  • 04

    A written data dictionary saves the next person's time.

    Documenting field definitions felt like overhead at the time, but meant the dashboard could be handed over or rebuilt in a "real" BI tool later without re-litigating what each number meant.
