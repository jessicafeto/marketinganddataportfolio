← Back to all projects Lumio · Sleep & recovery, DTC subscription Case study 05 Performance marketing

Rebuilding the paid acquisition engine and bringing CAC back under control.

Lumio's Meta and Google Ads accounts had drifted into a handful of "evergreen" campaigns with no testing structure and no separation between cold and warm audiences. I rebuilt the campaign architecture from the ground up and introduced a systematic creative testing framework cutting CAC by over a third while scaling spend.

Sector DTC / e-commerce wellness subscription
Client Lumio (fictional brand)
Role Performance marketing lead
Channels Meta Ads & Google Ads
01 The brief

Spend was going up. So was the cost of every new customer.

Lumio a DTC brand selling magnesium blends, sleep masks and an evening "wind-down" routine on a subscribe-and-save model had been running paid social and search ads for about 18 months. The account had grown organically into five or six "always-on" campaigns, each with a handful of ad sets that hadn't been refreshed in months. Over that time, blended CAC had crept up by roughly 40%, ROAS had slipped below break-even on some campaigns, and there was no clear distinction between budget spent finding new customers versus budget spent re-showing ads to people who already knew the brand. Nobody could say with confidence which £1 of spend was doing which job.

02 The objective

Rebuild the engine, then make it testable.

Restructure for the funnel

Split spend cleanly into prospecting, retargeting and retention/upsell, so performance could be read and budget reallocated stage by stage.

Make creative testing routine

Replace the "set and forget" approach with a weekly testing cadence: new hooks and formats in, underperformers out, on a consistent schedule.

Bring CAC back under target

Get blended CAC back to a level that made the subscription model's LTV math work without simply cutting spend and shrinking the business.

03 My role

Owning the accounts end to end.

I worked as Lumio's performance marketing lead across Meta Ads Manager and Google Ads: planning the campaign structure, briefing and testing creative with an in-house designer, setting and reallocating budgets, and reporting on performance weekly. The brief was broad "make paid work harder" so the first job was turning that into a structure that could actually be measured and iterated on.

04 Strategy

Two changes that did most of the work.

Almost everything else followed from these two structural decisions.

A

Full-funnel campaign architecture

Rebuilt the account into three clearly separated layers: prospecting (broad targeting and Meta Advantage+ to find new audiences, plus Google Search and Performance Max for high-intent demand), retargeting (site visitors, add-to-carts and abandoned checkouts, with messaging tailored to where someone dropped off), and retention/upsell (existing subscribers offers for bundles, gifting and referrals). Each layer got its own budget, its own creative brief and its own success metric, so a campaign was never being judged against the wrong job.

B

Hook-first creative testing

Introduced a standing weekly batch of 4–6 new creatives, each built around a different opening "hook" (problem-led, benefit-led, social proof, founder-style UGC) rather than just new visuals on the same script. Every creative ran for a fixed test window against clear CTR and CPA thresholds; winners were scaled into prospecting budgets, and the underlying hook not just the asset fed the next week's batch. This turned creative from a one-off production task into a repeatable input to the account.

05 Execution

From restructure to a weekly rhythm.

Campaign restructure & budget split

Rebuilt the Meta and Google accounts around the three-layer funnel and set an initial budget split of roughly 60% prospecting, 30% retargeting and 10% retention reviewed and adjusted every two weeks based on blended CAC and ROAS by layer.

Creative testing framework

Set up a lightweight brief template and naming convention so every creative was tagged by hook, format and funnel stage. This made it possible to see, at a glance, which hooks were working not just which individual ads.

Audience & targeting strategy

Leaned into broad targeting and Meta's Advantage+ audiences for prospecting (letting strong creative do the targeting work), while keeping retargeting audiences tight and recency-based. On Google, shifted budget toward Performance Max and high-intent Search terms, away from broad Display.

Reporting & optimisation cadence

Built a weekly performance review covering CAC, ROAS and CTR by funnel layer, plus a kill/scale list for creative. Bi-weekly, this fed into the budget reallocation conversation with the founder so decisions were made on a rhythm, not in reaction to a single bad day.

08 Learnings

What worked, what I'd change next time.

  • 01

    Structure makes testing possible.

    Most of the early gains came not from any single ad, but from being able to see the account in three clear layers. Once CAC and ROAS could be read by funnel stage, the conversation with the founder shifted from "is paid working?" to "which layer needs attention this week?"

  • 02

    Hooks compound individual ads don't.

    Treating the hook (not the finished asset) as the unit of learning meant a single winning idea could be reformatted across carousels, Reels and static posts. I'd push for an even faster turnaround on hook variants next time, since the best-performing hook is rarely obvious in advance.

  • 03

    Broad targeting rewards strong creative.

    Letting Advantage+ find audiences, while focusing human effort on creative quality, consistently outperformed narrower manual targeting but only once the creative testing pipeline was actually producing strong assets.

  • 04

    A fixed cadence catches problems early.

    The weekly review caught underperforming creative and budget drift well before it became expensive. With more lead time, I'd add a lightweight daily spend-pacing check alongside the weekly performance review, especially during the first few weeks of the new structure.
