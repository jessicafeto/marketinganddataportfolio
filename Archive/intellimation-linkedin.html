Building a thought-leadership engine for a Vertical AI platform, one LinkedIn post at a time.

A 5-pillar content framework that turned intellimation.ai's LinkedIn page into the company's primary channel for BFSI thought leadership — built, written and run solo across a 7-month content sprint.

Sector BFSI / financial services technology
Client intellimation.ai — Vertical AI
Role Content & social media lead (sole)
Timeline Mar – Oct 2025
01 — The brief

A category-defining product with no consistent voice on its main channel.

intellimation.ai builds Vertical AI — AI purpose-built for banking and financial services, spanning collateral management, prime brokerage, structured products, direct lending and corporate treasury. The buyers for this kind of product (Front Office, Middle Office, Controls and Operations leaders at banks and asset managers) live on LinkedIn — but the company's page had no structured content plan, no consistent cadence, and no single owner connecting day-to-day posts to the product story or the events calendar.

I was brought in as Marketing & Operations Consultant (via Noova Data Ltd) to take ownership of LinkedIn as a channel: plan it, write it, design it, and report on it.

02 — The objective

Turn an inconsistent feed into a structured, measurable thought-leadership channel.

Three concrete goals shaped the brief:

Consistent cadence

Move from sporadic posting to a steady, planned drumbeat of content tied to a 5-pillar framework covering every Vertical AI product line.

Event-aligned visibility

Use LinkedIn to amplify the company's conference and roadshow calendar (SRP Europe, the Collateral Management Summit, New York trips) before, during and after each event.

Measurable engagement

Grow impressions, clicks and engagement on organic posts, and make the page a credible top-of-funnel source for sales conversations.

03 — My role

The sole content & social lead.

I owned LinkedIn end-to-end: research and angle development, copywriting, visual formatting, scheduling, hashtag and tagging strategy, and performance reporting. I worked directly with leadership — including Chetan Joshi (Director of Pre & Post Trade Products) and Aaron Armstrong (Partner Sales) — to turn their conference appearances, client conversations and product milestones into posts, and coordinated with the wider team so posts were shared and amplified across personal networks too.

04 — Strategy

A 5-pillar framework mapped straight onto the product line.

Every post had to do double duty: build the "Vertical AI vs. general-purpose GenAI" narrative and reinforce one of the five product verticals, so the feed read as a coherent map of what intellimation.ai does — not a stream of disconnected announcements.

01

Collateral Management

Regulatory-compliance and post-trade efficiency stories — the most event-heavy pillar (SRP Europe, Collateral Management Summit).

02

Prime Brokerage

Front-to-back automation narratives aimed at bridging front- and back-office operations for buy- and sell-side firms.

03

Structured Products

NLP-driven term-sheet automation — positioning Vertical AI inside a $7tn market where unstructured data is the bottleneck.

04

Direct Lending

Origination, servicing and compliance workflows reframed as Vertical AI use cases for lending teams.

05

Corporate Treasury

Automation and intelligence for treasury operations — the newest pillar, used to open conversations with a broader buyer set.

Why this framework?

Five pillars = five reasons to follow the page, and five ready-made angles whenever sales needed a credible LinkedIn post fast.

05 — Execution

From a blank calendar to 28 posts in seven months.

Content calendar

Built a rolling monthly calendar mapping each of the 5 pillars against the events calendar — so a conference week (e.g. SRP Europe in March, the Collateral Management Summit at the Hilton London in April, or the New York trip in May) always had a "before", "during" and "after" post, plus a pillar-led thought-leadership post in the gaps.

Writing & formatting

Wrote every post in a consistent voice — bold-formatted pull quotes, scannable stat call-outs (e.g. "$5bn+ invested in data quality", "27,000 structured products") and a consistent sign-off CTA ("Book a demo at hello@intellimation.ai") so every post could do top-of-funnel work.

Cross-team amplification

Coordinated with Chetan Joshi, Aaron Armstrong and the wider team so leadership quotes and event recaps were shared and reposted across personal profiles — multiplying reach for the same core content.

Reporting

Tracked impressions, clicks, reactions, comments and reposts in LinkedIn's native analytics every month, and used that data to double down on the formats and pillars that were working (event recaps and milestone posts) and rework the ones that weren't (pure-stat posts with no story).

08 — Learnings

What worked, what I'd change next time.

  • 01

    Story beats stat.

    The single best-performing post was a short, human "we're heading to NYC" announcement — not a data-led thought-leadership post. Narrative and event-tied posts consistently out-performed pure statistic call-outs.

  • 02

    A clear CTA changes the outcome entirely.

    The Collateral Management Summit recap, with a direct "book a demo" link, drove a 51.7% engagement rate and 516 clicks from just over 1,000 impressions — by far the highest click-through of any post. I'd build a clear CTA into every single post going forward, not just event recaps.

  • 03

    Comments are the weak link.

    Across 28 posts I generated only 17 comments. Next time, I'd write more posts that end with a genuine question, and reply actively to drive two-way conversation — comments carry more weight in LinkedIn's algorithm than reactions.

  • 04

    Cadence dips hurt momentum.

    June and September were quieter months for both posting and impressions. With more lead time I'd batch-produce content ahead of busy periods so the feed never goes quiet between events.
