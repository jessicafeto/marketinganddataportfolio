The Project

A new AI product with no marketplace presence, no demo playbook and no launch plan. I built all three and took it live.

P.R.I.M.E. was intellimation.ai's prime brokerage AI agent product. Getting it in front of buyers meant listing it on Symphony, the messaging and workflow network used across global financial services, then giving the commercial team something repeatable to actually sell with.

I owned the product marketing side of that launch end to end: the marketplace listing, the product experience designed in Figma, the demo playbook that made the sales conversations repeatable, and the campaign that announced it. The work supported 40+ demos with Tier-1 institutions.

The Marketplace Listing

Writing and designing the product's first public shopfront on a network used across global financial services.

Product Naming Feature Mapping Competitive Positioning Listing Copy

I wrote and designed the P.R.I.M.E. listing: the product name, the description, how the features were grouped and explained, and how it was positioned against general-purpose AI tools. That last part carried most of the weight. Buyers in this market have seen a great many AI products, and the listing had to make the case for a purpose-built agent rather than a general model pointed at a financial workflow.

Designing the Product Experience

Over 20 wireframe screens covering the full integration flow, built to be demonstrated rather than admired.

Figma Wireframing User Flows Prototyping

I designed the agent experience from marketplace install through to live workflow automation: counterparty onboarding, margin management, legal agreements and dispute resolution, with six distinct agent states visualised so a buyer could see what the software would actually do at each point.

The prototype was not a design exercise. It became the primary visual aid in every demo, which meant it had to be legible to a middle-office director in a first meeting, not just internally coherent.

The Demo Playbook

A four-step structure that made 40+ bank meetings repeatable instead of improvised.

  • Context set. Institution brief, use case selection and audience seniority mapping before the meeting started.
  • Workflow walk. Figma prototype walkthrough covering agent states and the Symphony chat integration.
  • Q&A frame. Objection handling, the ROI narrative, and prepared answers for compliance and regulatory questions.
  • Leave-behind. A one-pager tailored to that institution, plus the follow-up sequence for the sales team.
The playbook is the part I would point to first. A single good demo depends on whoever is in the room. A structure that anyone can run, with the objections already mapped and the leave-behind already written, is what turns one good meeting into forty.

Launch Campaign & Partner Marketing

Announcing the product and using the partnership as a distribution channel.

Partner Marketing Co-branded Campaign Event Marketing Executive Comms

I ran the LinkedIn launch campaign around the product announcement with co-branded posts and executive tagging, and coordinated the partner spotlight at Symphony's Innovate showcase, which put the product in front of a room of more than 400 industry leaders in person and online.

The institutions reached through the playbook included JP Morgan, HSBC, Bank of America, Barclays and Citi, alongside asset managers and fund administrators. Every one of those conversations ran on the same structure, the same prototype and the same leave-behind format.

Reflection

What I would carry forward.

This is the project where product marketing, design and sales enablement stopped being separate jobs. The listing copy, the prototype and the demo script all had to say the same thing in three different formats, and they only worked because they were built together rather than handed between people.

What I would add is a feedback loop from the demos back into the listing. Forty conversations generated a great deal of information about which objections came up repeatedly, and that should have been rewriting the marketplace copy as it accumulated rather than sitting in my notes.
