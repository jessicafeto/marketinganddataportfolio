Building a Lifecycle Email Programme for a Luxury Beach Club on the Croisette.

A self-directed concept project built around Annex Beach Cannes the private beach club, restaurant, and nightlife venue on Boulevard de la Croisette. Luxury hospitality brands typically rely on word-of-mouth and social presence. This project asks what happens when email does the work of deepening guest relationships between visits, driving repeat bookings, and building a VIP tier that feels genuinely exclusive.

01 The brief

Luxury hospitality runs on relationships. Email is where relationships scale.

Annex Beach Cannes attracts an international crowd of high-net-worth guests, fashion and film industry professionals, and returning regulars particularly during Film Festival season. The brand has strong social presence and an impeccable physical environment. What it lacks is a structured way of staying in the conversation with guests between visits: no welcome flow after a first reservation, no post-visit nurture, no VIP programme communicated through email, and no seasonal event series to build anticipation for high-value bookings.

The gap: no lifecycle thinking

First-time guests made a reservation and received a confirmation. Nothing else. The moment that determines whether a guest becomes a regular their first experience and the 72 hours after was left entirely to chance.

The opportunity: high-value seasonal demand

Annex Beach hosts fashion brand takeovers (including Versace), Cannes Film Festival events, and summer dinner cabaret. These are high-margin, high-demand events ideal for an email-led early access and priority booking strategy.

The objective

Design a lifecycle email programme that turns a single reservation into a recurring guest relationship through welcome flows, post-visit follow-up, VIP tier communication, and a seasonal event series.

02 Brand context

What Annex Beach is, and who it's talking to.

The venue

A private beach club, boutique restaurant, and nightlife venue on the iconic Boulevard de la Croisette directly opposite the Carlton Cannes Hotel. Italian-Mediterranean dining from 10am to 2:30am, summer cabaret under the stars, and fashion brand collaborations that extend the brand beyond hospitality into culture.

The guest

Affluent international visitors (France, UK, Middle East, US), film and fashion industry professionals during peak Cannes events, and European regulars who return season after season. Average spend €100+ per person. Reservations are essential; walk-ins are rare and often unsuccessful in peak season.

Brand tone: what email must feel like

The physical experience of Annex Beach is effortless, warm, and quietly exclusive the email programme must match that. The tone rules I set for this concept:

  • Personal, not promotional write as if from the maître d', not a marketing team
  • Specific and contextual reference the guest's reservation, event, or last visit where possible
  • Never discounting exclusivity is the offer, not a percentage off
  • Short and confident luxury brands don't over-explain
  • French Riviera aesthetic in subject lines and preview text evocative without being clichéd
03 Strategy

Five flows that cover the full guest lifecycle.

The programme is built around the moments that most influence whether a guest returns: the anticipation before arrival, the 48 hours after a great experience, the lead-up to major seasonal events, and the recovery of guests who haven't visited in over a year.

A

Reservation-to-arrival series

From the moment a guest books, the experience begins. A confirmation email (personal, not template), a 48-hour pre-arrival guide with what to expect, what to wear, and what's on and a same-day welcome message with their table details. Builds anticipation and reduces no-shows.

B

Post-visit nurture flow

The 72 hours after a first visit are the most important window for building a repeat guest. A post-visit email goes out 24 hours after departure: a short personal note, an invitation to share feedback, and a soft prompt to book for their next occasion or event.

C

VIP early access programme

Guests who have visited three or more times or who have spent above a threshold are quietly elevated into a VIP tier. They receive priority access to cabaret dinners, fashion brand event evenings, and Cannes Film Festival reservation windows before they open publicly communicated through a distinct email voice.

D

Seasonal event series

Cannes Film Festival (May), summer cabaret launch (June), fashion brand takeovers (July–August), and end-of-season farewell dinner (September). Each event triggers an email series: announcement, early access invitation, last-availability reminder, and post-event wrap for those who attended.

E

Win-back for lapsed guests

Guests who have not visited or interacted in 12 months receive a gentle re-engagement series not a discount, but a reminder of what they experienced and an invitation to return for a specific occasion (a birthday, an anniversary, or a seasonal event they attended previously). The series runs for three emails over six weeks; after that, the contact is suppressed from marketing sends to protect deliverability.

05 Segmentation logic

Who gets which email, and why.

By visit frequency

First-time visitors enter the welcome & post-visit flow. Guests with 2+ visits receive the event series. Guests with 3+ visits or high spend are elevated to the VIP flow a different tone, different booking access, and different content.

By recency

Guests active within the last 12 months remain in the main event series. Guests who have not visited in 12+ months enter the win-back flow. Contacts who don't re-engage after three win-back emails are suppressed to protect deliverability.

By occasion type

The reservation system captures occasion data (business dinner, birthday, anniversary, leisure). Emails in the post-visit and win-back series reference the original occasion and suggest returning for the same or next occasion personalisation without requiring manual CRM input.

07 Learnings

What this project clarified about email for luxury brands.

  • 01

    Luxury email earns trust through restraint, not frequency.

    The biggest temptation in email marketing is to send more. For a brand like Annex Beach, the opposite is true every email should feel like the venue itself: considered, unhurried, and worth the reader's attention. I designed the programme around fewer but more meaningful sends, rather than maximising list touches.

  • 02

    Occasion data is the most valuable CRM field a hospitality brand can collect.

    Knowing a guest booked for their anniversary changes every subsequent email. That single field enables personalised win-back, occasion-triggered re-engagement, and anniversary repeat prompts all without complex machine learning. The programme is built so that occasion capture happens at booking, not retrospectively.

  • 03

    The pre-arrival email is the most underused asset in hospitality marketing.

    Most venues confirm a booking and then go silent until a reminder the day before. The 48-hour pre-arrival email setting expectations, building excitement, and communicating what makes the experience special is the single highest-ROI email in the lifecycle because it starts converting first-timers into regulars before they've even sat down.
