Identified the Most Valuable Guests Using Frequency and Spend Analysis Restaurant CRM

Most restaurants know who walked through the door. Very few know which guests drove the most revenue, which ones are about to stop coming, and what a single additional visit per month from their top-20% of guests would actually be worth. This concept project applies customer frequency and spend analysis to a restaurant dataset to answer those questions and to design CRM actions that retain the guests most worth keeping.

Type Concept project
Sector Restaurants / Food & Beverage
Tools Python · SQL · Customer Segmentation
Output Segmentation model · CRM action plan
01 The business question

Who are your most valuable guests and what would it cost to lose them?

Restaurants typically measure success at the transaction level: covers, average spend per head, revenue per table. What most miss is the customer-level view the insight that 20% of guests likely drive 60–70% of repeat revenue, and that losing a single high-frequency regular has a compounding effect that isn't visible in weekly P&L reporting.

This project builds the customer-level picture: segmenting guests by how recently they visited, how often they visit, and how much they spend then designing CRM actions for each segment.

02 Approach

RFM analysis adapted for F&B behaviour.

Recency

Days since a guest's last visit. Recent visitors are more likely to return; lapsed guests need a re-engagement trigger. Recency is the strongest predictor of whether a customer will come back.

Frequency

Number of visits in the analysis window. Frequent guests have a higher emotional connection to the restaurant and are more resistant to competitors. Frequency also flags guests who have suddenly stopped a leading indicator of churn.

Monetary

Total spend per guest. High monetary value guests are priority retention targets. This metric also identifies guests who come often but spend little (targets for upsell) vs. guests who spend big but rarely (targets for frequency incentives).

03 Segments & CRM actions

Four segments. Four different strategies.

01

VIP Regulars

Profile: High recency, high frequency, high spend.
CRM action: Priority reservation access, birthday/anniversary personalisation, chef's table invitations. Goal: make them feel known and valued without offering discounts they don't need.

02

Promising Guests

Profile: Recent visits, growing frequency, moderate spend.
CRM action: Second-visit incentive (e.g., complimentary starter on third visit). Goal: establish the habit before it becomes a conscious choice.

03

At-Risk Regulars

Profile: Previously frequent, not visited recently.
CRM action: Personalised win-back message referencing a previous visit or favourite dish. Urgency offer (limited time). Goal: bring them back before they're gone.

04

Occasional Visitors

Profile: Infrequent, low-to-moderate spend.
CRM action: Event-led re-engagement (seasonal menu launch, special occasion). Low-cost broadcast approach. Goal: convert one-off visits into occasional visits.

Why F&B retention analytics matters

Acquiring a new restaurant guest costs 5–7× more than retaining an existing one. A restaurant group with 10,000 customer records in its booking or POS system is sitting on a segmentation dataset most just haven't analysed it. This project demonstrates the ability to build that analysis from raw data, produce actionable segments, and design CRM responses that a marketing or operations team can actually run with.
