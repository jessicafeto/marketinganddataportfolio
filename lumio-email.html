← Back to all projects Lumio · Sleep & recovery, DTC subscription Case study 06 Email & lifecycle marketing

Turning a monthly newsletter into a lifecycle programme that pays for itself.

Email was Lumio's most underused channel one generic newsletter a month to the entire list, and no automated flows at all. I built the core lifecycle architecture in Klaviyo welcome, abandoned cart, replenishment and win-back and segmented the list so each email earned its place in someone's inbox.

Sector DTC / e-commerce wellness subscription
Client Lumio (fictional brand)
Role Email & CRM marketing lead
Platform Klaviyo
01 The brief

One newsletter a month, to everyone, about everything.

Lumio had a list of several thousand subscribers and one email programme: a monthly newsletter covering new products, blog content and the occasional promotion, sent to the entire list regardless of whether someone was a brand-new visitor, a loyal subscriber on their tenth order, or someone who'd abandoned a cart six months ago. There were no automated flows no welcome series for new sign-ups, nothing triggered by browsing or cart behaviour, and no replenishment reminders despite the subscribe-and-save model being central to the business. Email was contributing under 10% of revenue, well below what's typical for a list of that size.

02 The objective

Build the flows that should already exist.

Stand up core lifecycle flows

Welcome series, browse & cart abandonment, post-purchase/replenishment and win-back the foundational automations every e-commerce brand needs, none of which existed yet.

Segment the list properly

Split subscribers by engagement, purchase status and subscription stage, so campaigns could be relevant instead of one-size-fits-all.

Grow email's share of revenue

Move email from a "nice to have" newsletter to a measurable, meaningful share of total revenue with flows doing most of the heavy lifting.

03 My role

Flow design, copy, segmentation and testing.

I led email and CRM marketing for Lumio in Klaviyo: mapping out the lifecycle flows that were missing, writing the copy and structuring the email designs, building the segmentation that fed both flows and campaigns, and running an ongoing subject-line and send-time testing programme. I also worked through a list cleanup to protect deliverability before scaling send volume.

04 Strategy

Flows first, then make them personal.

With nothing automated in place, the priority was getting the foundational flows live then layering segmentation on top so each flow felt targeted rather than generic.

A

Foundational flow architecture

Built four core flows in priority order: a welcome series for new subscribers (brand story, best-sellers, first-order incentive), browse & cart abandonment (triggered reminders with increasing urgency, including a soft discount on the final email), post-purchase & replenishment (set-up guidance, then timed reminders aligned to subscription cadence so people reordered before they ran out), and win-back for subscribers who paused or churned (re-engagement offer, then a "what changed" survey if there was no response).

B

Segmentation & personalisation

Split the list into segments by engagement (active vs. dormant openers, used to protect sender reputation), purchase status (never purchased, one-time buyer, active subscriber), and subscription stage (new, established, at-risk based on skip/pause behaviour). Campaigns and flow content were tailored per segment a "why subscribe" message went to one-time buyers, while active subscribers saw loyalty and referral content instead.

05 Execution

From flow maps to a tested, healthy programme.

Flow mapping & build

Mapped each flow as a simple diagram (trigger → wait → email → decision) before building in Klaviyo, so stakeholders could sign off on the logic before any copy was written.

Subject line & content testing

Ran ongoing A/B tests on subject lines and send times across the welcome series and weekly campaigns, rolling winning variants into future sends rather than treating each test as one-off.

Segmentation build

Built the engagement, purchase-status and subscription-stage segments as dynamic groups in Klaviyo, so every new subscriber and order automatically updated who saw what.

Deliverability & list hygiene

Suppressed long-term unengaged subscribers from regular sends, ran a re-engagement campaign before removing them entirely, and monitored open/spam-complaint rates to keep the list healthy as send volume increased.

08 Learnings

What worked, what I'd change next time.

  • 01

    Flows beat campaigns especially early on.

    The welcome series alone outperformed every campaign send, simply because it reached people at the moment they were most interested. Building flows first, before investing more time in one-off campaigns, was the right call.

  • 02

    Replenishment timing has to match real usage.

    The first version of the replenishment flow used the subscription's billing date, but some customers used product faster or slower than that. I'd build in a simple "how's your supply?" check-in earlier next time, rather than relying purely on billing cadence.

  • 03

    List hygiene pays for itself.

    Suppressing unengaged subscribers felt counterintuitive a smaller list but it protected deliverability for everyone else and made open-rate trends much easier to read and act on.

  • 04

    Segmentation is only useful if content actually differs.

    Early on, segments existed but most emails still said the same thing to everyone. The real lift came once content not just targeting was written differently for subscribers vs. one-time buyers.
