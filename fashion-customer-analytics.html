Fashion Retail Customer Analytics Segmentation, Churn Prediction & Retention Strategy

An end-to-end Python analysis of 29,730 fashion retail records: RFM segmentation to identify distinct customer groups, five ML models to predict churn, and actionable retention recommendations grounded in the data. Academic thesis work that demonstrates exactly the kind of customer intelligence a marketing team needs to stop guessing and start targeting.

01 The brief

Fashion retail runs on gut feel. This project asked: what does the data actually say?

Most fashion retailers hold rich transactional data but lack the analytical infrastructure to turn it into customer strategy. The brief: use a real-world fashion dataset to build the kind of customer intelligence that informs targeting, retention, and lifecycle decisions the work a marketing analyst or customer insight team would do before a campaign brief is written.

Who are the customers?

Segment the customer base into meaningful groups based on purchasing behaviour, engagement, and value not just demographics or product category.

Who is about to leave?

Build a churn prediction model that identifies at-risk customers before they lapse giving marketing teams enough lead time to act.

What should marketing do differently?

Translate the segment and churn findings into specific campaign, CRM, and retention recommendations that a marketing team could act on.

03 Methodology

Three analytical layers: exploration, segmentation, prediction.

The analysis moved through three phases understanding the data, grouping customers into meaningful segments, and then building models to predict which customers were most at risk of churning.

Phase 01

Exploratory Data Analysis

Distribution analysis of numerical features (Price, Rating, Review Count, Age) and categorical breakdowns across brands, categories, and style attributes. Identified key patterns and data quality issues before modelling.

Phase 02

RFM Segmentation

Scored customers on Recency (how recently they bought), Frequency (how often), and Monetary value (how much they spent). RFM scores were used to cluster customers into behavioural segments with distinct marketing implications.

Phase 03

Churn Prediction Modelling

Trained and evaluated five machine learning classifiers to predict which customers were most likely to churn. Models were assessed on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score with a focus on correctly identifying at-risk customers before they lapse.

Python code: RFM scoring (excerpt)

# Compute RFM scores from transaction history import pandas as pd from datetime import datetime snapshot_date = datetime(2024, 1, 1) rfm = df.groupby('CustomerID').agg({ 'PurchaseDate': lambda x: (snapshot_date - x.max()).days, 'OrderID': 'count', 'Revenue': 'sum' }).rename(columns={ 'PurchaseDate': 'Recency', 'OrderID': 'Frequency', 'Revenue': 'Monetary' }) # Quantile-based scoring: 1 (worst) to 5 (best) rfm['R_Score'] = pd.qcut(rfm['Recency'], 5, labels=[5,4,3,2,1]) rfm['F_Score'] = pd.qcut(rfm['Frequency'], 5, labels=[1,2,3,4,5]) rfm['M_Score'] = pd.qcut(rfm['Monetary'], 5, labels=[1,2,3,4,5]) rfm['RFM_Score'] = rfm['R_Score'].astype(str) + rfm['F_Score'].astype(str) + rfm['M_Score'].astype(str)
04 Customer segments

Five distinct customer groups each with a different marketing response.

The RFM analysis produced five behavioural segments. Each has a different relationship with the brand and a different marketing action that follows from it.

Segment 01

Champions

Bought recently, buy often, and spend the most. These customers love the brand. They are the priority for loyalty rewards, early access to new drops, and UGC/advocacy programmes.

High R High F High M
Segment 02

Loyal Regulars

Spend consistently and buy often, but not always the most recent purchasers. They respond well to exclusive member benefits, category recommendations, and seasonal campaign targeting.

Mid R High F Mid–High M
Segment 03

Potential Loyalists

Recent buyers who show high engagement but haven't yet built purchase frequency. The segment most responsive to onboarding nurture, product education, and a second-purchase incentive.

High R Low F Mid M
Segment 04

At-Risk

Previously active customers who haven't purchased recently. Their recency score is declining. These customers overlap most with the churn model's high-risk predictions the primary target for win-back campaigns before they fully lapse.

Low R Mid F Mid M
Segment 05

Lapsed

Low recency, low frequency, low monetary value. Most have not purchased in the longest period. Some will respond to a compelling re-engagement offer; others have likely moved to a competitor. Focus on cost-efficient re-activation, not full campaign spend.

Low R Low F Low M
05 Churn prediction models

Five models evaluated. Random Forest performed best on the metric that matters.

For churn prediction, recall is the priority metric the cost of missing an at-risk customer is higher than the cost of a false alarm. Five classifiers were trained and compared.

Logistic Regression

Baseline model. Interpretable and fast. Performs well on linearly separable data but misses non-linear churn patterns in complex purchase histories.

Decision Tree

Fully interpretable every prediction path can be traced. Prone to overfitting without pruning. Useful for communicating decision logic to non-technical stakeholders.

Random Forest

Best overall performer. Ensemble of decision trees reduces overfitting, handles mixed feature types well, and produces reliable feature importance scores identifying which customer signals most predict churn.

K-Nearest Neighbours

Strong on well-structured, normalised feature sets. Computationally expensive at scale and sensitive to irrelevant features less practical for production deployment on large customer databases.

Support Vector Machine

Effective at finding a clean boundary between churned and retained customers in high-dimensional space. Slower to train than tree-based models and less interpretable for business stakeholders.

Key insight from the feature importance analysis

The Random Forest model identified purchase recency and review engagement as the two strongest predictors of churn more predictive than product category or price point. Customers who stopped leaving reviews before stopping purchases showed the earliest churn signal.

07 Business recommendations

What a marketing team should do with these findings.

1. Build a churn alert into the CRM

The At-Risk segment (Segment 04) should trigger an automatic win-back flow when recency drops past a threshold not after the customer has already fully lapsed. Earlier intervention costs less and converts better than win-back emails sent to Lapsed customers.

2. Differentiate campaigns by RFM segment

Champions should receive early access and loyalty recognition, not the same promotional email as first-time buyers. Sending the same campaign to all five segments is one of the most common reasons email marketing underperforms in retail.

3. Use review engagement as an early warning signal

The model found that customers who stop engaging with review content before stopping purchases are showing an early churn signal. A targeted content engagement trigger not a discount should fire at the point of review disengagement.

4. Personalise by style cluster, not just category

Style attribute (Streetwear, Vintage, Formal, Sporty) proved more predictive than product category. Campaign personalisation by style preference in copy, imagery, and product selection is likely to outperform category-based targeting.

09 Learnings

What I'd approach differently, and what this work taught me.

  • 01

    The most valuable output is the business recommendation, not the model.

    A 92% accurate churn model is not the end result it's the starting point for a conversation with a marketing team. The work only lands if the findings are translated into actions that the business can actually run. I'd spend more time on the recommendation layer in future projects.

  • 02

    Segment names matter more than segment numbers.

    Cluster 3 means nothing to a campaign manager. "Potential Loyalists" tells the team exactly who they're talking to and what the opportunity is. Naming and describing customer segments in plain English is one of the highest-value things a data analyst can do for a marketing team.

  • 03

    Review engagement is an underused signal in fashion retail.

    Most fashion retailers track purchase recency but not content engagement recency. The finding that review disengagement precedes purchase lapse is commercially useful and it's the kind of insight that requires cross-channel data rather than just transaction logs.
