Building Noova's Brand Through Content A 6-Month Strategy for a Marketing Studio.

Noova My Ltd is my marketing and creative studio delivering brand systems, social media, email marketing, and CRM for consumer and lifestyle brands. This project is the content strategy I'm building for Noova itself: how to grow the studio's own audience, establish authority in consumer marketing, and turn content into a consistent source of inbound interest from the right clients.

Studio Noova My Ltd
Type Forward-looking strategy
Channels LinkedIn & Instagram
Horizon 6 months
Goal Inbound from consumer/lifestyle brands
About this project: Noova My Ltd is my own marketing studio. This is an active, forward-looking content strategy a plan for what Noova's content will become, built using the same methodology I apply to client briefs. The approach, pillars, and calendar below are real strategy, not a hypothetical exercise.
01 The brief

Noova does excellent work. It needs to be seen doing it.

Noova was built to serve consumer and lifestyle brands beauty, hospitality, fashion, wellness. The work is strong. But a studio's reputation is only as wide as the audience that knows about it, and right now Noova's reach is limited to direct referrals and personal network. The brief: build a content strategy that makes Noova's expertise visible to the clients it wants to work with, without sounding like every other agency posting generic marketing tips on LinkedIn.

The problem with most agency content

Most marketing agencies post about themselves awards, new hires, capabilities decks disguised as carousels. None of it proves you can actually do the thing. The Noova content strategy is built around showing the work, not selling the studio.

The target client

Founders and marketing managers at consumer and lifestyle brands (beauty, hospitality, fashion, wellness) in London. They are looking for a studio that understands their sector, not just marketing in the abstract. They need to see evidence before they pick up the phone.

The objective

Within six months: grow LinkedIn followers by 60%, establish a consistent Instagram presence for the studio, and generate at least two inbound enquiries per month that reference the content. Content should do the qualification work attracting the right clients, not just any clients.

02 Audience mapping

Two audiences. Two jobs for the content to do.

Primary: decision-makers at consumer brands

Founders, CMOs, and marketing managers at beauty, hospitality, fashion, wellness, and restaurant businesses in London. Typically 28–50. Looking for a marketing partner who gets their sector without needing to be briefed on it. They follow marketing content when it gives them ideas they can actually use not when it's self-promotional.

What they need to see in Noova's content: Sector expertise, analytical thinking, and case studies that look like their clients and problems. They need to trust before they enquire.

Secondary: marketing community and peers

Other marketers, brand managers, and content professionals. They share and amplify good content. They are a referral network, not a client pool. Their engagement increases reach and signals credibility to the primary audience.

What they need to see: Original thinking, data-backed insights, and opinions that aren't just rehashed from marketing blogs. They share what teaches them something they didn't already know.

03 Content pillars

Five pillars. Each earns a different type of trust.

Each pillar serves a distinct role in the content mix some build authority, some demonstrate execution, some expand reach.

01

Brand breakdowns

Deep-dive analysis of a well-known consumer brand their positioning, CRM, content strategy, or campaign mechanics. Not opinion, but structured analytical thinking applied to real brands.

Example: "Why Glossier's email strategy earns 3x the industry open rate and what it's built on."
02

Marketing analytics insights

Data-led takes on consumer marketing trends what the numbers actually show, not what the headlines claim. Python, GA4, and CRM data used to answer questions marketing teams care about.

Example: "We analysed 3 months of email data from 5 beauty brands. Here's what open rate actually tells you (and what it doesn't)."
03

Case study excerpts

Single-insight posts pulled from Noova's case studies one specific finding, recommendation, or result. Not a portfolio pitch; a proof of thinking. Always with a transferable lesson for the reader.

Example: "The email metric that predicted churn 6 weeks before customers stopped buying and what we did about it."
04

Sector trend commentary

A take on what's shifting in beauty, hospitality, fashion, or wellness marketing informed by what Noova sees working with clients and what the data shows. Quick, opinionated, and backed by something specific.

Example: "Aesthetics clinics are about to get a lot more competitive online. Here's the one brand asset most of them are missing."
05

Studio process & behind the scenes

How Noova approaches a brief, builds a strategy, or delivers a campaign. The least promotional content but often the most memorable because it gives clients a window into how the studio thinks before they commit to working with it.

Example: "How we audit a brand before touching any creative. The six questions we ask first."
04 Channel strategy

LinkedIn and Instagram. Different audiences. Different content formats.

LinkedIn authority channel

Where Noova earns professional credibility

LinkedIn is the primary channel for reaching decision-makers. The content here is longer-form, more analytical, and more explicitly professional. Posting cadence: 3× per week.

  • Brand breakdowns and marketing analytics insights (pillars 01 & 02)
  • Case study excerpts with structured findings (pillar 03)
  • Sector trend commentary aimed at marketing professionals (pillar 04)
  • One studio process post per month (pillar 05)
  • No generic "5 tips" content every post earns its place with specificity
Instagram brand & visual channel

Where Noova shows its aesthetic sensibility

Instagram is where the studio's visual identity lives and where consumer brand founders browse for agency fit. Content here is visual-first, shorter in copy, and shows the quality of Noova's creative output. Posting cadence: 4× per week.

  • Design and brand asset showcases (Framer builds, email templates, brand systems)
  • Short-form campaign breakdowns in carousel or Reel format
  • Brand audit snapshots condensed to three slides
  • Studio culture and process in Stories
  • Consistent visual style: dark backgrounds, gold type, editorial photography feel

07 Principles

The rules this strategy is built on.

  • 01

    Show the work. The studio follows.

    The fastest way to attract a client who values strategic thinking is to publish strategic thinking publicly. Every post that demonstrates how Noova analyses a brand, diagnoses a marketing problem, or builds a campaign is doing qualification work that no pitch deck can replicate. The content is the portfolio, not just a pointer to it.

  • 02

    Specificity is what separates content that builds trust from content that fills a grid.

    Generic marketing advice is everywhere. What is not everywhere is a post that says "here is the specific CRM field that changed every email we send hospitality clients" and then explains exactly what it is and why it matters. The more specific the content, the more it signals expertise and the more it earns the trust of clients who have been burned by agencies that were only good at seeming impressive.

  • 03

    Consistency compounds. The best content strategy is the one you can sustain for six months.

    The strategy above is achievable at three LinkedIn posts and four Instagram posts per week designed for one person (me) with existing material (the case studies) to draw from. A strategy that requires constant net-new research would collapse by week four. The pillar framework is built so that case study material generates content across multiple pillars without starting from zero each time.
